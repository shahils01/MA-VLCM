# Actor and VLM critic: held-out formation evaluation

The 60 new Isaac Lab episodes in `Ground_Robot_MARL/datasets/formation_validation_seed7300001` were generated by the saved intermediate on-policy MAPPO actor. The actor was frozen, used its robot observations to choose actions, and did not receive the critic value. The separate MA-VLCM critic at `MA-VLCM/outputs/zero_margin_900/best.pt` (epoch 5) then performed fresh inference on these archived observations. The SmolVLM backbone was frozen during its training; the graph, fusion, temporal and value layers were trained. Neither model was trained during this evaluation. The videos replay those saved inference results.

The same actor drove all four formations, with 15 new episodes and a fresh simulator stream per formation. The formations and actor stage occurred in critic training, so these are new initial scenarios for known formations, not unseen-shape or unseen-policy generalization.

MSE is over nonterminal clips and weights longer episodes more. The overall MSE is 0.05710, MAE 0.16104 and R² 0.8160 over 2,498 clips. Pentagon successes show more error than its failures in this sample. Terminal rewards and final terminal images were omitted in these videos at the user's request; they remain intact in the underlying archives and full-return labels.

## From prediction-only to training the actor

The current test does not establish that the critic improves the actor. The Python formation runner (`MARL_data_collection/on-policy/onpolicy/runner/shared/runner_formation_control.py`) currently calls its own MAPPO actor and state critic to collect rollout values, then computes returns/advantages and updates both models. The actor-only `act` path uses the actor and does not need a critic for deployment. MA-VLCM `best.pt` is a separate checkpoint and is not a drop-in replacement for the runner's `R_Critic` weights or observation interface.

A direct VLM-in-the-loop experiment requires: (1) a tested adapter that constructs the critic's causal image/state/adjacency histories from Python rollouts, with no future or outcome labels; (2) held-out evaluation of Python-rendered images and aligned robot-state features for visual/domain shift; (3) matching team reward, terminal semantics, and per-control-step discount; (4) an explicit way to supply valid values at the 20 Hz MAPPO/GAE cadence or a mathematically consistent interval-level estimator. The existing critic was trained from 5 Hz frame anchors and gamma 0.99 per control step; the formation MAPPO configuration uses gamma 0.995. Holding a 5 Hz value constant for four 20 Hz steps is not a validated substitute. (5) Update the value head on current actor rollouts, or evaluate a frozen baseline while tracking bias; as the policy changes its value can change. PPO actor actions and log probabilities must remain those actually collected.

A state-only student distilled from the VLM on Isaac trajectories is a lower-cost training baseline that avoids Python-image mismatch, but the student is a surrogate critic; it is not direct VLM-in-the-loop MAPPO. Compare actor training against the existing state-critic MAPPO baseline using equal environment steps and multiple seeds, and evaluate actor-only checkpoints in Isaac on matched held-out scenarios. Measure success, return, safety/collisions, and formation error as well as critic errors. No VLM-assisted actor training has been run in this evaluation.
